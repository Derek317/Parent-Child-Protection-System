# Parent-Child-Protection-System
# Convoy (HK) — All-in-One Parental Protection System for Instagram

Convoy was called IG Guard (IG Teen Guard) up to 6.0.3. It is an independent app for Instagram, not made or approved by Meta.

**Convoy** is a single-APK Android family safeguarding app for Instagram users who meet Instagram's applicable minimum age: at least 13, or higher where required. It helps a parent or legal guardian review captured text and selected risk signals. It does not guarantee safety, complete capture or detection of every harmful interaction.

Current version: **6.0.4**, documented **28 November 2026 (Hong Kong)**: 6.0.3 plus fixes from an external bug review and a follow-up audit, see the [6.0.4 release notes](docs/releases/6.0.4.md). **6.0.3**, documented **26 November 2026 (Hong Kong)**: 6.0.2 plus eleven fixes from a second audit (delivery warning, child records, relay, password screens, scoring), see the [6.0.3 release notes](docs/releases/6.0.3.md); 6.0.2 itself is 6.0 plus fixes for twelve audited problems, see the [6.0.2 release notes](docs/releases/6.0.2.md). Bundled Terms revision `2026-11-26-v10`. What 6.0 itself changed is in the [6.0 release notes](docs/releases/6.0.md) and the [changelog](CHANGELOG.md). 6.0 replaces chat invitations with a one-time QR code scanned in person, gives the child phone its own signing key, locks the relay room to that key, and warns the parent when the child's records stop arriving. Both phones must be updated, and families paired on 5.2 keep the old risk until they pair again by QR code. No consent step, existing warning or security measure was removed. The [5.2 notes](docs/releases/5.2.md) describe the previous version. The [5.0 notes](docs/releases/5.0.md) still describe the app and its limits.

**Installing.** On phones where Google Play Protect's enhanced fraud protection is active, installing the downloaded APK by opening it from a browser, messaging app or file manager is blocked, with no "install anyway" option, because the app declares an accessibility service and a notification listener. Installing over USB with `adb` from a computer is not blocked. There is no Google Play listing. Upgrading from 5.0 or 5.1 keeps data (same signing key).

Public review references **E01/E02** are available in the [dated evidence index](docs/reviews/2026-11-16/evidence/README.md). These are release-verification and prior cleanup records with stated limits, not a legal certification or a claim that the app has no flaws.

## Purpose & Core Objectives

1. **Approved-Contact DM Alerts:** A parent-controlled approved-friends list is independent of Instagram follow status. Captured incoming messages from unapproved account labels generate encrypted alerts. An unapproved label does not establish a sender's age, identity or intent, and capture is not comprehensive.
2. **Keyword-Based Text Alerts (English + HK Cantonese):** Rules flag selected sexual or grooming-related phrases. The child app attempts to leave flagged chats and clear their notifications while forwarding captured follow-ups. This does not classify images/audio, understand the full grooming process or block the account on Instagram's servers.
3. **IG Mute Detection & Protected App Controls:** Detects Instagram mute/quiet-mode screens and protects in-app role changes with parent verification. Android controls its own permission settings and uninstallation; the reporting watchdog warns when a previously connected child stops reporting.

## Family use and message privacy

Convoy is restricted to authorised private parent–child safeguarding. Do not use it to monitor strangers, partners, employees, students or for commercial/institutional surveillance. Hong Kong Cap.486 s52 is a limited exemption for qualifying personal data held by an individual for personal/family/household affairs or recreational purposes; it does not automatically exempt the developer or Cloudflare, grant Meta permission or authorise every family use.

On first launch on **each phone**, a native offline page displays the exact [Terms of Service](docs/ig-guard-terms-of-service.md) bundled from this repository. Three unticked acknowledgments explain the Terms, conditional child-mode message transfer to the verified paired parent/legal guardian, and the family-only purpose. Capture, pairing and relay delivery remain off until acceptance, including after upgrading an older configured phone. Decline or Back returns to Android Home without recording acceptance; existing data is retained and queued transfers are held. Acceptance is saved as a device-bound encrypted receipt with revision, SHA-256 and UTC time. Ordinary launches and updates do not repeat the initial page; Settings → 使用條款與同意記錄 provides review. Clear app data or reinstall and acceptance is required again. Initial acceptance does **not** enable expanded two-way capture or grant Android permissions. Material new processing scope requires separate consent; an old receipt is not rewritten as agreement to new Terms.

There is no school administration console or developer-operated plaintext family message archive.

**The developer-deployed Cloudflare relay handles ciphertext and delivery metadata; it is not supplied the family decryption key.** Captures are encrypted on the child phone and decrypted and archived on the paired parent phone. In the optional scores-only mode (AI risk analysis) no message text is sent: the parent receives only an account label, a risk score and text-free alerts (see the [Terms](docs/ig-guard-terms-of-service.md), sections 2A to 2C). Connected phones normally exchange encrypted packets through the relay immediately; undelivered packets and critical controls use temporary encrypted cloud mailboxes. There is no developer plaintext-message dashboard or key escrow. APK signing does not provide family encryption keys. The original 4.8 APK uses ntfy; both phones need the verified Cloudflare hotfix build to migrate.

The developer has administration access to the relay’s ciphertext and metadata. Cloudflare also processes connection information. This is a managed backend, not a direct phone-to-phone connection. Buffered relay packets expire seven days after queuing and are removed sooner when consumed; a still-unconfirmed record on the phone may be resubmitted. Room registration metadata is deleted after 90 days without a connection from either phone, or sooner when an authenticated parent deletes the room in the app; the room is then retired for good, with only a content-free marker kept. Provider logging, hosting and backups remain unverified. Keep family pairing invitations/tokens private: they contain the secret used to derive the message-encryption key.

Archive content and preference strings are encrypted with device-bound Android Keystore keys. Sender/thread lookups use keyed indexes; timestamps, categories, opaque IDs and workflow flags remain visible metadata. Existing records migrate on first open; keyword search decrypts candidate rows in the app. Encryption does not protect an already compromised running app or exported copies. See [local storage security](docs/local-storage-security.md).

New parent archive records receive a 30-day expiry by default; phone outboxes and incomplete assemblies have 72-hour deadlines. Record details offer explicit, one-time 90-day preservation, distinct from the ordinary saved/bookmark flag. Existing archives remain outside automatic expiry until the parent reviews a preview and confirms adoption. Expired content is cleaned during app activity; an app that is stopped cannot promise immediate on-disk removal. See the [release notes](docs/releases/5.0.md). Deleting a local record does not remotely erase relay copies or copies a user has made.

Incoming-alert mode excludes outgoing DMs. Separately acknowledged expanded capture can include exposed incoming and sent outgoing messages and ordinary trusted-contact context; the child can choose outgoing and trusted-context scope separately. Composer contents and descendants remain excluded; layout/hint metadata can identify a DM screen. Earlier draft observations remain labelled historical and unconfirmed as sent, subject to archive controls. Encrypted exchanges also include relevant account labels, trusted-contact settings, timestamps and device/protection status.

On the child's phone, **擷取範圍與停止** first sends the parent one content-free notice (waiting up to about 6 seconds for the relay), then stops new capture and local pending transfer persistently, with a choice to discard pending content or hold it within its existing deadline. Explicit resume discards old pending content and restarts incoming alerts; expanded capture requires a fresh child acknowledgment. Saving scope choices also discards old pending content, and widening scope requires renewed acknowledgment. Android permissions can additionally be disabled; turn off both **Notification access** and **Accessibility** to remove both capture paths. Already forwarded or relay-buffered copies can still reach the parent and need separate handling. See the [Privacy Policy](docs/ig-guard-privacy-policy.md), [Terms](docs/ig-guard-terms-of-service.md) and [deletion instructions](docs/ig-guard-data-deletion.md).

Parent records and controls require native Android biometric/device-credential authentication and relock when the activity leaves the foreground. This UI lock does not stop background receipt, decryption, archiving or acknowledgments. Secure-window flags and generic private notifications reduce incidental exposure; clipboard copies are marked sensitive and the app attempts to clear its still-current item after 60 seconds. Copies, shared device credentials and compromised devices remain outside these protections.

Production parent commands use non-exportable Android Keystore signing identities. A verified legacy import preserves the public key already trusted by the child; only after verification is the encoded private-key preference removed. Mismatched identities fail closed without silently replacing trust. If moving an existing key into the Keystore fails, the app keeps using that existing key (stored encrypted by the app, not hardware-protected), shows a warning and retries at each start; the old copy is removed only after the Keystore key is verified to match the key the child trusts. Routine remote rotation uses the existing channel and is **not compromised-key recovery**. For suspected compromise, **家長主密碼與金鑰 → 秘密可能外洩：重新安全配對** creates fresh secrets and signing identity for trusted local verification and re-pairing. The screen can first send the child phone a signed release and ask the relay to delete the old room, and reports whether the relay confirmed it; otherwise it keeps the existing pairing and offers creating a new pairing only, which gives no relay-deletion assurance. Old local archives stay isolated; old pending content and trust settings are discarded. Replacement secrets do not travel through the suspect old channel. Stop the child's old capture locally and re-pair both safe phones with the QR code and the 6 digits; copied old data cannot be recalled. See the [release notes](docs/releases/5.0.md).

Convoy is independent of Meta and has no Meta approval. Official Instagram API and portability integrations are not implemented. Current capture uses Android notifications and accessibility, and depends on what Instagram exposes. In the scores-only mode a family can instead choose manual upload, where the app reads nothing from Instagram and the teen imports Instagram's own data export.

Not yet checked on a phone: Instagram Teen Accounts silence notifications overnight by default, so notification-based capture may see nothing at night. Manual upload of Instagram's own export is not affected.

See the [code-backed data-flow description](docs/privacy-data-flow.md) and [preliminary Hong Kong legal assessment](docs/hong-kong-privacy-assessment.md). These describe the implementation and questions to resolve; they are not legal certification. Android permission grants, a parent-role selection and encryption alone do not establish lawful authority to monitor someone.

## Key Features & Architecture

| Feature Category | How It Works | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **All-in-One Parent & Child APK** | Both parent and child install the exact same APK and select **Parent Mode** or **Child Mode** during onboarding. Child monitoring hooks (`NotificationListenerService` & `AccessibilityService`) only run in Child Mode. | Eliminates third-party notification apps (such as `ntfy`) and provides a built-in **Parent Live Alert Inbox** and native high-priority Android notifications. |
| **QR Pairing & Authority Verification** | New pairings use a one-time QR code shown on the parent phone and scanned in person by the child phone: new each time, valid 3 minutes, single use, holding a one-time secret and the parent's public key and no long-term secret. The two phones then agree a new family secret between themselves; it is never shown, typed or sent through a chat app. The child phone shows 6 digits, the parent types them (3 tries) and accepts, confirming with the parent phone's own screen lock or biometric, and the child confirms on its own phone; four pictures are a visual aid only. If two phones answer the same QR code, pairing stops. Chat invitations (pairing text or link) are no longer offered for new pairings, and a child phone refuses them. Pairings made before this change keep working and show a permanent "not verified" notice on the parent phone until both phones are paired again with the QR code. A child phone is re-paired only with the parent password (or after a signed remote reset), and the old family is sent a tamper alert first. The child phone asks for camera permission only on the scan screen, only to read the QR code; no image is stored or sent. | Reduces accidental family collisions and removes the long-lived secret that a chat invitation used to carry. It does not prove that the phone in front of the parent is the child's own phone. |
| **Child Device Key & Signed Child Records** | The child phone creates its own non-exportable signing key in the Android Keystore and signs every record it sends to the parent. Once the parent has verified a child phone this way, a record without a valid signature from that key is refused, nothing from it is stored, and the parent gets a content-free notice (at most one per hour). For a family paired this way the relay stores the child phone's public key (no content, no private key) and admits a child connection only with proof of that key. Families not paired this way are handled as before. | Closes the impersonation risk that was open through 5.2 for pairings made by QR code. A rooted or compromised child phone, a parent who pairs the wrong device, a child who scans a stranger's QR code, uninstalling the app, relay metadata and the lack of hardware key attestation remain; see [security-review.md](docs/security-review.md). Not yet tested on real phones. |
| **End-to-End Encryption (`AES-256-GCM` over TLS)** | Encrypts every alert, heartbeat, allowlist sync, unlock code, and key-rotation command with **AES-256-GCM** (random 96-bit IV + 128-bit auth tag) before leaving the phone. The 256-bit key is the SHA-256 of a family token with at most 128 bits of randomness, so the protection is about 128-bit, not 256-bit (still far beyond brute force), then exchanges encrypted packets over authenticated WSS. | Protects message contents in transit; the relay is not supplied the family secret needed to decrypt them. Protection depends on keeping pairing secrets and devices secure. |
| **Notification and visible-conversation capture** | Two-way mode processes notification message batches and exposed incoming/outgoing bubbles in an identified Instagram DM thread. Composer bodies and descendants are excluded; the typed comment/story-reply sender is removed. Unknown authorship/direction stays labelled unknown. | Supplies captured two-way context for parent review. It cannot retrieve hidden history, guarantee all outgoing messages in the background or reproduce an official personal-account inbox API. Incoming-alert behavior remains until the expanded scope is acknowledged. |
| **Flagged Offenders Watchlist (`已封鎖高危帳號`)** | Accounts associated with selected keyword matches enter the watchlist. The child phone attempts to leave those chats (`GLOBAL_ACTION_BACK`) and clear notifications, while forwarding captured follow-ups within the active consent scope. | Retains available follow-up text for parent review; labels do not establish intent and capture and delivery can be incomplete. |
| **IG Mute Detection & Protected App Controls** | In child mode, the Instagram-only accessibility service backs out of detected mute/quiet screens and sends alerts. Parent authentication protects the app’s role-change and unlock controls. | Android Settings and uninstallation remain system-controlled. The app reports permission states and detects overdue reporting after the first authenticated child report. |
| **Random 30–95 Min Heartbeat & Offline Watchdog** | The Child Phone sends an encrypted heartbeat at random 30–95 minute intervals (30–65 while Notification access is off or not connected, where only the 15-minute job sends) reporting permission states. The Parent Phone triggers an overdue-reporting alert if more than **100 minutes** pass without a heartbeat after a prior authenticated report. | Warns about interrupted reporting; it cannot determine whether the cause is shutdown, connectivity, revoked permissions or uninstallation. |
| **12+ Char Master Password, 15-Min Unlock Code & Remote Rescue** | • A new **Parent Master Password** needs 12+ characters with a letter and a digit, and common passwords are refused; the setup screen recommends the generated password. Passwords set before this rule keep working.<br>• Parent Phone generates a random 6-character **15-Minute Temporary Unlock Code**, delivered in a signed encrypted command (with 5-try brute-force lockout).<br>• Unlock codes are single-use. Unlock expiry, code life and the wrong-password lockout are timed on the device's monotonic clock with a reboot marker (an unlock ends at reboot, a reboot restarts a lockout), so changing the clock cannot extend them; a child whose clock moves back more than 5 minutes alerts the parent.<br>• Includes signed **Remote Key Rotation** and **Remote Child Reset** commands (the parent phone asks for a fresh device authentication before sending them, before showing the master password and before leaving the parent role); failed rotations retain retry state. The child stores a password verifier instead of the parent’s plaintext password. | Protects against PIN guessing, lets parents grant temporary 15-minute access without revealing their Master Password, and allows remote unlock if the Master Password is forgotten. |

## Repository Structure & Cloud APK Build

This project compiles directly in the cloud via **GitHub Actions** with zero local Android Studio setup required:

* `MainActivity.kt` (or `MainActivity_v4.kt`): Kotlin source containing `GuardEngine`, `IgNotificationListener`, `IgAccessibilityService`, `ParentAlertService`, and `MainActivity`.
* `.github/workflows/build-apk.yml`: Assembles the Android Gradle project, uses private signing credentials from GitHub Actions Secrets when configured, and auto-increments `versionCode` so future updates install in-place without uninstalling the previous version.
* `ui-res/`: Vector icons, light/dark themes and sheet animations used by the role-first family interface; copied into the generated Android project by the workflow. See [asset licences](UI_ASSET_LICENSES.md).

Role choice is a one-time page: a phone with no role opens on it, while a phone that already has a role opens on its normal first screen, and existing protection is retained until an authorised role change. Parent and child each have four navigation sections. Charts, alerts, connection states and family settings use real stored data; new installations have an empty trust list.

* `FamilySecurity.kt`: Native password-verifier and parent-command authentication primitives; no third-party runtime dependency.
* `third_party/`: ZXing core 3.5.3 (Apache-2.0), used only to draw and read the pairing QR code; the build refuses it unless its SHA-256 matches the pinned value.
* `SecurityControls.kt`: Parent UI authorization state and sensitive clipboard handling; background delivery is independent of the UI lock.
* `FamilyEvidence.kt`: Native SQLite evidence archive, encrypted delivery outbox and bounded long-message framing; no third-party runtime dependency.
* `ConversationCapture.kt`: Conversation records, source/direction metadata, rule-match labels, validation and bounded visible-snapshot comparison.

Read [security migration](docs/security-review.md) before updating existing installations. V43 invitations (older pairings; new pairings use a QR code) keep the parent password and signing private key off child phones; parent controls require signatures and temporary codes require delivery. [Private signing setup](docs/signing.md) replaces the exposed historical signing key. Until replacement secrets are configured, Actions produces a clearly labelled unsigned APK requiring private signing before installation; only `main` and release tags are ever signed.


## Evidence archive and reliable delivery

- The parent archive uses native app-private SQLite with the expiry and legacy-adoption rules above. A DM-style list groups records by conversation partner/account, with latest recorded activity first. Within each conversation, 30-record pages run chronologically, with older-message navigation, direction/source labels, search and saved records. Saving alone does not extend retention. Known source message time is used when available; otherwise capture/legacy record time is explicitly labelled.
- Conversations, actionable review items and device/delivery events have separate destinations. Ordinary messages do not raise per-message alerts or inflate alert charts. Review items have New, Follow-up and Reviewed states; reviewing an old item does not review a later arrival. Keyword categories and exact matching phrases are signals for human review, not findings of grooming.
- Both phones must support the new record schema. A signed parent request and local acknowledgment on the child phone are required before expanded capture starts. A software update alone does not enable the broader scope. Parent setup is available from the conversation section; the child can inspect the scope in protection settings.
- Existing retained JSON records migrate once and are marked as possibly truncated, including before a family change. Unparseable legacy data is preserved as a clearly labelled raw recovery record. Previously discarded records or truncated characters cannot be recovered. Instagram may expose only a notification snippet or a portion of the visible screen; this is an archive of **captured text**, not a complete or certified forensic conversation record.
- Long captured text is encoded as bounded encrypted parts with stable IDs and a full-payload digest. The transport safety bound is 1 MiB per serialized alert. Complete assemblies, deletion tombstones and queued ciphertext are durable; malformed/conflicting parts are rejected. Incomplete assemblies are identified in the parent interface.
- Failed uploads back off and retry through the existing child-service lifecycle within the pending deadline and active consent gate. Securely paired updated phones use signed parent receipts: relay acceptance alone does not clear a message waiting for parent confirmation, and waiting for one receipt does not delay a new alert. Receipts confirm parent-device receipt, not that an adult read or acted on an alert. The Cloudflare relay requires a pinned parent key. Upgrade older invitations through the normal adult-authorized pairing flow; expiry and deliberate pending-discard controls still apply.
- Update **both phones together** for long-message framing and signed receipts. A parent using the older app cannot reassemble the new long-message parts. The managed relay's free account quotas, retention and availability are external limits; no monitoring can run after Android stops the app, required access is revoked or the app is uninstalled.
- Keyword alerts offer calm guidance to speak with the child and seek an appropriate school social worker or safeguarding professional. They are signals, not findings of abuse. Background alerts use Android notifications with generic lock-screen content, not overlays. Hong Kong immediate danger: 999.
- In acknowledged expanded mode, displayed outgoing DMs and ordinary trusted-contact messages are included only within the child's scope choices. Unapproved incoming contacts still generate review items; an outgoing keyword match cannot itself flag the other party as its author. Unsent comment/story-reply capture remains removed; older draft records remain historical. No microphone, screenshot or photo-library capture is added.
- Backups and device transfer remain disabled for app data. The archive is on this phone: uninstalling or clearing app data removes it. A new family does not see the old family's records; routine key rotation within the family retains history and eligible queued delivery through a recoverable transition journal without extending pending deadlines. Old isolated archives can be explicitly removed in parent settings. Fresh compromise recovery is a separate authorization and must not reuse this old-channel transition.

Existing capture, alerts, archives, search, review states, trusted contacts, pairing, temporary unlock, routine rotation and background delivery remain part of the app, subject to the deliberate authentication, consent and retention gates. The rules pipeline itself uses no model. A model (Beacon, about 1.1 GB) runs on the child's phone only in the optional scores-only mode, and nothing shows that it reliably detects real grooming. The model file is not in this git repository. The app published on this repository's GitHub Releases page includes it (the model file is also published on GitHub Releases as an asset of a separate release (`model-beacon`), so it is publicly downloadable); on first start a child phone copies it into the app's private storage, which needs about 1.1 GB free on top of the installed app. Ordinary builds of `main` have no model, and AI risk analysis cannot be used on them until a model is present; manual import with the file picker is a recovery path only. See [local-model-package.md](docs/local-model-package.md). The release APK is over 1 GB: it installs directly, but Google Play's legacy APK limit is 100 MB, so a Play listing would need an app bundle with an asset pack, which is not built. Third-party licences for the engine and the model are in [THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md](THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md). Any `LiquidAI/d1-omni-600M` feasibility work is a separate experiment using synthetic scenarios; this documentation makes no claim of model efficacy or measured Android feasibility.

This update improves retry/storage behavior; it does not certify Play Protect/banking acceptance or prove Instagram capture on every phone/version. The app cannot silently grant Android special access or make a bank trust an accessibility service.

## Cloudflare relay deployment

The approved migration replaces ntfy with a free Worker and SQLite-backed Durable Objects. Normal upgrades preserve V43 public-key trust and local history; child withdrawal, fresh scope acknowledgment and pending expiry still apply. Update both phones together. A relay connection or publish acceptance is separate from a signed parent-phone receipt. This documentation update does not deploy any relay change.

See [setup and deployment](docs/cloudflare-relay-setup.md), [capacity assumptions](docs/relay-capacity-assessment.md), the historical [migration verification report](docs/relay-migration-report.md) and the [5.0 release notes](docs/releases/5.0.md) for current hardening limits. On every push the workflow tests the relay. Only a push to `main` or a hotfix release tag (for example `4.8-hotfix.N` or `6.0.3-hotfix.N`) deploys it and then runs a live authenticated offline exchange against the production endpoint before packaging. Only `main` and release tags are signed; the workflow verifies the signing certificate on those builds before a tagged release is published, and every other ref produces an unsigned artifact kept for seven days. This protects the signing key only after the repository secrets are moved into protected environments; see [CI secrets hardening](docs/ci-secrets-hardening.md). If a release-environment approval is rejected on a tag, the build fails and the workflow still publishes the documented notes-only release (never an APK); see [CI secrets hardening](docs/ci-secrets-hardening.md). Beta tags are published as prereleases and do not replace the stable latest release. A free plan has finite shared quotas; a load simulation is not a guarantee of unlimited production capacity.
