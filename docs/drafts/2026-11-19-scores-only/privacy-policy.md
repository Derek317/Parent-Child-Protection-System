## Initial choice on each phone

Each phone displays the complete bilingual [Terms of Service](ig-guard-terms-of-service.md) offline on its initial consent page. The APK bundles the exact UTF-8 GitHub document as `ig-guard-terms-of-service.md` and displays it verbatim. The three confirmations start unticked, in this order: reading and agreeing to the full terms; conditional encrypted transfer to the dedicated verified paired parent or legal guardian with the disclosed security and retention; and authorized family-only use with the child/parent informed, without treating section 52 as automatic legal permission. Acceptance requires all three and an explicit Accept action. A separate local `ig_guard_consent` preference store holds an AES-GCM-encrypted receipt containing the acceptance date, app version, terms revision and SHA-256 of the exact terms bytes. It is not an automatic support upload or a certification of legal authority.

The optional scores-only family mode has its own, separate agreement by the child on the child's phone; see "Scores-only family mode" below. It is not one of the three confirmations and does not replace this initial choice.

Collection and relay are disabled until that phone accepts. Decline or Android Back returns to Android Home without creating an acceptance receipt. Existing local queues are held pending acceptance within their deadlines, not erased by declining. Previously forwarded relay or parent copies are not deleted by declining. A parent phone and a child phone each make their own initial choice; a parent's acceptance does not provide consent on behalf of every correspondent.

Normal later launches and updates do not automatically repeat this initial page. Settings provides read-only review; materially new scope needs separate consent. Existing receipts retain their original revision and hash, not agreement to new Terms. Acceptance does not grant Android permissions, enable expanded capture or cancel a child's persistent stop choice. Pairing, Android permissions, a signed parent request and separate child-phone acknowledgment for expansion still apply. A normal update alone does not revoke an existing scope acknowledgment; stopping, resuming or widening scope requires fresh acknowledgment before expansion resumes.

## What IG Guard does

IG Guard pairs a teen’s Android phone with a dedicated parent or legal guardian’s Android phone for authorized private family safeguarding. Surveillance of strangers, partners, staff, students or clients, and commercial or institutional surveillance, is prohibited. After initial acceptance, pairing and the relevant Android permissions in Child Mode, and while collection is active, the app processes Instagram notifications and selected text exposed on the phone’s screen. It uses that information to flag selected words and messages from contacts outside the family’s approved list, forward selected records encrypted to that paired parent or legal guardian for decryption and reading (in the optional scores-only family mode, scores and content-free alerts are sent instead of message text), and report whether protection and delivery are working. Acceptance supplies no missing pairing or Android permission. The app uses Android services for capture. Instagram passwords and Meta API access tokens are outside this flow.

Use is limited to Instagram accounts meeting Instagram's applicable minimum age: at least 13, or higher where required. IG Guard is independent of Meta and has no Meta approval. Official Instagram API and portability integrations are not implemented.

In incoming-alert mode, the app sends qualifying incoming alerts. Expanded capture requires a signed request from the parent device and a separate acknowledgment on the teen’s device. The child can separately choose sent outgoing messages and ordinary trusted-contact context within that expanded scope. Disabling ordinary trusted context does not suppress an otherwise qualifying safety alert from a trusted contact. Composer drafts remain excluded, including comment/story-reply text and DM composer bodies; layout/hint metadata may identify a DM screen. Captures depend on the notifications and view Instagram exposes, so records may be incomplete or have uncertain author or account labels. There is no guarantee of complete capture or protection. No model is included in the app. A model file installed by hand runs on the child's phone only in the optional scores-only family mode, described below. Any LiquidAI/d1-omni-600M synthetic-data feasibility experiment is separate and does not establish detection efficacy.

Older builds could forward unsent comment/story-reply drafts. Those retained records remain labelled historical, with sending unconfirmed. The update does not automatically delete them; normal archive deletion controls still apply.

## Scores-only family mode (optional)

This mode is off by default. A parent switches it on in the parent phone, and the switch stays disabled until the child's phone has reported a valid model file. Nothing is stored or analysed until the child agrees once, on a separate page on the child's phone. If the child has not agreed, the parent sees "waiting for your child to agree" and cannot agree for the child; the earlier mode keeps running. IG Guard is independent of Meta and has no Meta approval; this mode uses the existing capture, and official Instagram API and portability integrations are not implemented.

**What is analysed.** One-to-one Instagram conversations that the app already captures through notifications and screen text. Group chats and conversations of unknown type are not stored or analysed; they are counted in the report. The app may not be able to tell the type of many captured screen conversations, and those are not analysed.

**Where analysis runs.** On the child's phone, using a model file (about 1.1 GB) installed by hand. The app checks its size and SHA-256 and refuses any other file. It runs while the phone is idle and charging, or on charging alone if no run has happened for 48 hours. In this mode no chat text is sent to any server or to the parent's phone. The phone needs about 8 GB of memory; otherwise the mode is unavailable.

**New encrypted store on the child's phone.** Captured one-to-one messages are kept in an encrypted store using the same device-bound keys as the existing local vault. It keeps the latest 60 messages per conversation and nothing older than 14 days. Each conversation is scored on its latest 30 messages, and the reported score is the highest seen in the last 14 days. This store is new: before this mode, the child's phone kept no readable copy of messages. It is deleted when the child withdraws. [To be confirmed: whether it is also deleted at once when the parent switches the mode off, when the model file is removed, or in other cases.] Clearing app data or uninstalling removes it.

**What the parent receives.** For each conversation, the contact's account label, a score from 0 to 100 or "needs review", and the time. Content-free alerts carry the rule category, the account label and the time, with no message text, quote, summary or matched phrase. The parent also sees the child's status: model state, agreed or waiting, paused and when, withdrawn, time of the last run, the number of conversations scored and the number skipped because their type is unknown. These records use the existing end-to-end encrypted relay, and wait in the phone's queue if the relay is down. The parent's phone keeps the scores it receives, and the parent may delete them [deletion rules to be confirmed against the build].

**The child's controls.** The app always shows that analysis is on, when it last ran and how many conversations were scored. The child can pause at any time; this takes effect at once, including a run in progress, and stops storing as well as scoring. The parent is told as soon as the relay is reachable and sees "paused by your child" and when. Resuming needs no new consent. The child can withdraw: this deletes the store and any unsent scores and tells the parent. Scores the parent already received stay on the parent's phone. Switching the mode on again needs the child's agreement again. After withdrawal the phone does not fall back to forwarding text; the parent sees "withdrawn by your child", and only the parent switching the mode off returns the family to the earlier mode.

**Families already paired.** After the switch, the parent no longer receives new message text. The parent's existing archive stays until the parent deletes it. Both phones show what will change before the switch.

**The other person in each conversation.** Their messages are stored and analysed on the child's phone, and their account label is sent to the parent. They have not been asked to agree, and the agreement of the parent or child does not speak for them. They can use the contact address below.

**Limits of the model.** Tested only on invented conversations; no real conversation has been scored. It can miss concerning conversations and wrongly flag harmless ones. A score is a signal, not a finding. Capture is incomplete, so scores cover only what was captured. Parents cannot read messages in this mode, even in an emergency. No promise is made about detection accuracy or safety outcomes. Speed, battery use and heat on real phones have not been measured.

## Scope choices and withdrawal

The teen can open **擷取範圍與停止** on the child phone and persistently stop new capture and local pending transfer, choosing to discard pending content or hold it within its original 72-hour deadline. Explicit resume discards held old pending content and restarts incoming alerts; it does not resend the old queue or restore expanded capture without renewed child acknowledgment. Saving outgoing/trusted-context choices also discards old pending content; widening a choice clears the expanded acknowledgment. Android grants are not automatically revoked by this stop control. In scores-only family mode the child also has Pause and Withdraw, described above.

Both IG Guard **Notification access** and **Accessibility** can additionally be disabled in Android Settings. Turning off only one leaves the other capture path available. Permission revocation alone does not discard queues; use the in-app stop control to hold local transfer. Stopping cannot recall content already forwarded or buffered at the relay, which may still arrive, or delete delivered archives and external copies. Parents can read delivered text and choose to copy it elsewhere. Those copies are managed by whoever holds them.

## Access to parent records and keys

The parent UI requires native Android biometric/device-credential authentication and relocks when it leaves the foreground. Background delivery, decryption, archiving and signed receipts continue while that UI is locked. The app uses secure-window flags and generic private notifications, subject to Android/device behavior. Explicit app copies are marked sensitive and the app attempts to clear only its still-current clipboard item after 60 seconds; process termination, background restrictions or copying elsewhere can defeat that cleanup. Selected text copied through Android or shared outside the app is outside that timer's control. Someone who can use the phone's enrolled credentials may gain access.

Production parent-command signing uses non-exportable Android Keystore keys. Verified migration imports the existing legacy key against the child's pinned public key before removing its encoded private-key preference. Software signing helpers used for tests are not a production storage guarantee. The parent password is still retained in encrypted preferences where existing reveal/pairing functions need it; the child stores a verifier. Hardware backing varies, and an unavailable or mismatched signing identity is not silently replaced. Routine rotation uses the existing channel and does not recover from its compromise. **家長主密碼與金鑰 → 秘密可能外洩：重新安全配對** creates fresh secrets and signing identity without sending replacement secrets over that channel. ## Storage, retention and deletion

- **Parent phone.** New archive records receive a default 30-day deadline based on record time, with shorter retention supported. Existing archives remain without an expiry until preview and confirmed adoption; adoption can immediately expire older content. Record details offer explicit individual preservation for a one-time 90-day period from that action. Ordinary saved/bookmark or review flags do not extend retention. Cleanup occurs during app activity and archive processing; a deadline is not assurance of immediate on-disk removal while the app is stopped. Archive deletion can leave chart counts and replay-prevention identifiers. Message content and labels use device-bound Keystore AES-GCM field encryption with HMAC lookup indexes; timestamps, categories, opaque IDs and workflow flags remain visible. Preference strings, including the parent password where needed and family secret, are encrypted at rest. The app decrypts during use; Android app isolation and device security remain necessary. Losing Keystore keys can make data unreadable, without a silent reset.
- **Phone pending content.** Encrypted outboxes and incomplete assemblies receive 72-hour deadlines. Retries and routine rotation do not renew them; expiry/discard metadata prevents the same local event from being requeued. Existing pending rows get a 72-hour transition deadline on schema migration. Unaccepted or withdrawn collection holds local delivery without extending its deadline; explicit resume discards old pending content. Expired content may remain on disk until cleanup executes. Clearing app storage removes that device's queue, settings and pairing.
- **Child phone, scores-only store.** Latest 60 messages per one-to-one conversation and nothing older than 14 days, encrypted with device-bound keys. Deleted on the child's withdrawal, together with unsent scores. Pause stops storing and scoring [to be confirmed: whether messages already stored are kept while paused]. Clearing app data or uninstalling removes it. The relay and Safe Guard never receive it.
- **Parent phone, scores.** Scores, alerts and child status received in this mode. The plan applies the archive's deletion rules to them [to be confirmed: whether the 30-day default applies]. A child's withdrawal does not delete scores already received. The parent's existing message archive is not deleted when the mode is switched on.
- **Consent receipt on each phone.** The local receipt has no automatic expiry; it persists across normal launches and updates. Clearing that phone's app data or uninstalling removes it. Archive-record deletion does not withdraw acceptance or erase the separate receipt.
- **Cloudflare relay source.** Packets receive 72-hour lifetimes from first acceptance; consumed mailbox ciphertext is removed sooner, and duplicate submissions do not renew the stored deadline. Existing seven-day mailbox entries are shortened using their original enqueue time. Content-free packet replay metadata can remain for 90 days after expiry. Room registration metadata (parent public key, family scope, child capability hash and authorization replay state) retires after 90 days without qualifying activity; older registrations start that idle period when the new policy is adopted. Authenticated parent retirement/purge deletes that room's queues and registration and closes connections, retaining only a retirement marker for a further 90 days. The recovery flow offers this request; local archive deletion alone does not, and offline recovery does not confirm purge. These source changes need separate deployment verification.
- **Provider and support records.** Cloudflare logging, hosting regions and backups have not been independently verified. No fixed support-correspondence retention period has been verified. Packet, registration and retirement deadlines do not establish those records' lifetimes or guarantee forensic erasure.

## Family use and Hong Kong law

Section 52 of Hong Kong's Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance (Cap. 486) concerns personal data held by an individual and concerned only with the management of his personal, family or household affairs, or so held only for recreational purposes. It exempts qualifying data from the Data Protection Principles, Parts 4 and 5, and sections 36 and 38(b), rather than every provision of the Ordinance. Whether a particular use qualifies depends on its facts. It does not automatically exempt Safe Guard's or Cloudflare's processing. Encryption and a family-use label do not settle that assessment.

Explain actual capture and forwarding to the teen and obtain the authority and consents required for the circumstances. Other correspondents retain applicable rights and privacy interests; in scores-only mode their messages are held on the child's phone for up to 14 days and their account label reaches the parent; other laws and Instagram's contractual rules continue to matter. Neither this notice, initial acceptance, Android permission nor a scope acknowledgment is legal certification or blanket permission to process others' information. See the [Terms](ig-guard-terms-of-service.md), [storage boundaries](local-storage-security.md) and [current draft notes](releases/4.9-beta.4.md). Earlier data-flow reports describe their dated source versions.
