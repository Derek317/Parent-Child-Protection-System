# IG Guard Privacy Policy

Updated: **24 November 2026 (Hong Kong)**. This notice describes **IG Guard R1**, bundled Terms revision **2026-11-24-v8**. See the [release notes and known limits](releases/6.0.md). Safe Guard operates the project and can be reached at [ppefyp2026@gmail.com](mailto:ppefyp2026@gmail.com).

## Initial choice on each phone

Each phone displays the complete bilingual [Terms of Service](ig-guard-terms-of-service.md) offline on its initial consent page. The APK bundles the exact UTF-8 GitHub document as `ig-guard-terms-of-service.md` and displays it verbatim. The three confirmations start unticked, in this order: reading and agreeing to the full terms; conditional encrypted transfer to the dedicated verified paired parent or legal guardian with the disclosed security and retention; and authorized family-only use with the child/parent informed, without treating section 52 as automatic legal permission. Acceptance requires all three and an explicit Accept action. A separate local `ig_guard_consent` preference store holds an AES-GCM-encrypted receipt containing the acceptance date, app version, terms revision and SHA-256 of the exact terms bytes. It is not an automatic support upload or a certification of legal authority.

Collection and relay are disabled until that phone accepts. Decline or Android Back returns to Android Home without creating an acceptance receipt. Existing local queues are held pending acceptance within their deadlines, not erased by declining. Previously forwarded relay or parent copies are not deleted by declining. A parent phone and a child phone each make their own initial choice; a parent's acceptance does not provide consent on behalf of every correspondent.

Normal later launches and updates do not automatically repeat this initial page. Settings provides read-only review; materially new scope needs separate consent. Existing receipts retain their original revision and hash, not agreement to new Terms. Acceptance does not grant Android permissions, enable expanded capture or cancel a child's persistent stop choice. Pairing, Android permissions, a signed parent request and separate child-phone acknowledgment for expansion still apply. A normal update alone does not revoke an existing scope acknowledgment; stopping, resuming or widening scope requires fresh acknowledgment before expansion resumes. The optional scores-only mode (below) has its own agreement on the child's phone, separate from the three confirmations; it records the agreed collection method.

## What IG Guard does

IG Guard pairs a teen’s Android phone with a dedicated parent or legal guardian’s Android phone for authorized private family safeguarding. Surveillance of strangers, partners, staff, students or clients, and commercial or institutional surveillance, is prohibited. After initial acceptance, pairing and the relevant Android permissions in Child Mode, and while collection is active, the app processes Instagram notifications and selected text exposed on the phone’s screen. It uses that information to flag selected words and messages from contacts outside the family’s approved list, forward selected records encrypted to that paired parent or legal guardian for decryption and reading (except in the scores-only mode below, where no message text is forwarded), and report whether protection and delivery are working. A child phone also checks in in the background about every 15 minutes: it sends a heartbeat when one is due and asks the relay for parent commands, even when both Notification access and Accessibility are off. Android may delay this under battery saving. Acceptance supplies no missing pairing or Android permission. The app uses Android services for capture. Instagram passwords and Meta API access tokens are outside this flow.

Use is limited to Instagram accounts meeting Instagram's applicable minimum age: at least 13, or higher where required. IG Guard is independent of Meta and has no Meta approval. Official Instagram API and portability integrations are not implemented.

In incoming-alert mode, the app sends qualifying incoming alerts. Expanded capture requires a signed request from the parent device and a separate acknowledgment on the teen’s device. The child can separately choose sent outgoing messages and ordinary trusted-contact context within that expanded scope. Disabling ordinary trusted context does not suppress an otherwise qualifying safety alert from a trusted contact. Composer drafts remain excluded, including comment/story-reply text and DM composer bodies; layout/hint metadata may identify a DM screen. Captures depend on the notifications and view Instagram exposes, so records may be incomplete or have uncertain author or account labels. There is no guarantee of complete capture or protection. The app released on GitHub Releases includes a model; it runs on the child's phone only in the scores-only mode below. Any LiquidAI/d1-omni-600M synthetic-data feasibility experiment is separate and does not establish detection efficacy.

Older builds could forward unsent comment/story-reply drafts. Those retained records remain labelled historical, with sending unconfirmed. The update does not automatically delete them; normal archive deletion controls still apply.

## AI risk analysis (scores-only mode, optional)

On the child's phone this is called **AI 風險分析 / AI risk analysis**. Section 2A to 2C of the [Terms](ig-guard-terms-of-service.md) describe it in full. In short:

- **Who decides.** The parent can switch it on only after the child's phone has reported a valid model. The child must agree on their own phone; the parent cannot agree for them, and nothing changes before the child agrees. Every parent change needs a fresh device authentication on the parent phone.
- **Where it runs.** One-to-one conversations are analysed on the child's phone by a local model, only while the phone is charging. No message text is sent anywhere for analysis.
- **What the parent receives.** For each conversation: the other person's account label, a score from 0 to 100 or "needs review", and the time; alerts without message text; and whether analysis is on, paused or withdrawn. The parent does not receive message text in this mode, even in an emergency. This holds whatever the two-way capture options are: in this mode they only decide what the child phone itself stores for analysis.
- **Outside this mode** the earlier behaviour is unchanged: captured messages are forwarded encrypted to the paired parent, incoming only, or incoming plus sent outgoing messages once the child has confirmed the two-way capture scope.
- **Collection method.** The parent chooses, the child agrees, and a change needs the child's fresh agreement. *Automatic capture* reads Instagram notifications and conversation screens; the parent must tick a warning that this may breach Instagram's Terms of Use and that the child's account could be restricted or disabled, and the child's consent page repeats it. The warning informs; it does not by itself remove anyone else's legal responsibility. *Manual upload* reads nothing from Instagram. The teen imports the file from Instagram's "Export your information" (a .zip, or one message_N.html / message_N.json). The file is read as a stream and not copied into the app; only message text, direction, time and conversation label of one-to-one chats are kept, and group chats are skipped. Real-time keyword and unapproved-contact alerts are not available in this method. The file comes from the child, so the parent cannot know that it is complete or unedited.
- **Export guide, reminder and optional repeating export (manual upload).** The child's upload card links to the steps for Instagram's own "Export your information" tool and has a button that opens its fixed address (https://accountscenter.instagram.com/info_and_permissions/dyi); the address carries no data about the child or the family, and the child does the export inside Instagram's own screens. The file picker opens at Downloads and lists zip, JSON and HTML files, and the app still checks the file's content. While upload is agreed and analysis is on, the child phone shows at most one content-free reminder a day when the last successful import (or the agreement, if none yet) is more than 7 days old; it appears on the child phone only and is never sent to the parent. For this the child phone keeps only the time of the last successful import, in its encrypted store, deleted together with the stored chat text. The parent can also ask for a fresh export at most once every 24 hours: the request carries no content, the parent is not told whether it was delivered or read, the child may ignore or dismiss it without anything being blocked, and the child phone keeps only the time of the last request shown, and whether its card was closed, in its encrypted store: the card goes when the child dismisses it or after a successful import, and the time stays to limit repeats and is deleted together with the stored chat text. After a successful import, analysis starts at the next charge without waiting for the phone to be idle; it still runs only while charging. Optional links open Instagram's own "Export to external service" set-up for a weekly export to the child's own Google Drive or Dropbox. That is Meta's feature, not IG Guard's: a copy of the messages then sits in the child's own cloud drive under that provider's terms and must be deleted there separately; Instagram asks for the Instagram password and IG Guard never sees or receives it; IG Guard does not read the cloud drive, and import still goes through the same file picker. The app shows this explanation and asks for confirmation before such a link opens.
- **Capture hold.** If the child agreed to manual upload and the parent later switches the mode off, the app does not go back to reading Instagram until the child allows it on their own phone. The parent is told that capture is held.
- **The other person.** Their messages are stored and analysed on the child's phone, and their account label reaches the parent. They are not asked.
- **The model** is "Beacon" (Qwen3 1.7B with a trained add-on, about 1.1 GB, run by llama.cpp). The source repository does not contain the model file. The app released on the repository's GitHub Releases page includes the model, and the model file is also published there as a release asset, so anyone can download it; other builds have no model, and AI risk analysis cannot be used on them until a model is present. On first start on the child's phone the app copies the model into its private storage (about 1.1 GB of free space is needed on top of the installed app). Manual import is a recovery path only. Any file whose size and SHA-256 do not match is refused. Phones reporting less than about 5 GB of memory, or with an unsupported processor, show the mode as unavailable. It was trained and tested on invented conversations, a small number of real conversations supplied by the developer, conversations from the PAN-12 research corpus (a public research collection of real chats) and the developer's own ordinary chats. None of that real material is stored as files in the app or the repository, and the conversations are not stored as files inside the model. A trained model can sometimes reproduce fragments of its training text, including text written by other people in those conversations. A limited test by the developer found no reproduction of the real training conversations; it used one setting and one prompt length, did not include the PAN-12 conversations, cannot show whether someone could tell that a particular conversation was used in training, and cannot prove that the model never reproduces training text. Because the model is published for anyone to download, this risk is greater: anyone with the file can try to draw such fragments out, and a downloaded copy cannot be recalled. **Open question:** the lawful basis and consent for using real conversations in training has not been settled and is with a Hong Kong privacy lawyer to answer.
- **Limits.** The model can be wrong in both directions and cannot reliably tell sexual conversation between adults from grooming. Nothing shows that it reliably detects real grooming. A score is a signal, not a conclusion. If you believe a child is in immediate danger, call 999 (Hong Kong).

## Pairing

Section 2D of the [Terms](ig-guard-terms-of-service.md) describes this in full. In short:

- **QR code, in person.** The parent phone shows a one-time QR code and the child phone scans it. It is new each time, valid for 3 minutes and works once. It holds a one-time secret and the parent phone's public key and no long-term secret. The two phones then agree a new family secret between themselves; it is never shown, typed or sent through a chat app. The child phone shows 6 digits, the parent types them on the parent phone (3 tries) and accepts, confirming with the parent phone's own screen lock or biometric, and the child confirms on its own phone. Four pictures on both phones are a visual aid only. If two phones answer the same QR code, pairing stops.
- **No chat invitations for new pairings.** A child phone refuses an old invitation text or link.
- **Signed records.** The child phone's Keystore signing key cannot be exported. Every record it sends to the parent is signed. After the parent phone has verified a child phone, a record without a valid signature from that key is refused and nothing from it is stored; the parent gets a notice without content, at most one per hour. A correctly signed record that is too old, dated too far ahead or of an unexpected kind is dropped quietly.
- **Delivery check.** The signed heartbeat of a child phone paired by QR code also carries two counters and one flag about reports not yet confirmed as delivered to the parent phone: how many, how long the oldest has waited, and whether one ever expired undelivered. They hold no content and no sender. The parent phone uses them to show a notice without content, which normally can take up to about 2.5 hours after delivery stops, and at most about 3 hours plus one heartbeat interval if the parent phone's own connection keeps dropping. Other control records (such as capture acknowledgements) do not wait for the parent phone's receipt, so the notice does not cover them.
- **Scores reports wait for a receipt.** Scores reports from a child phone paired by QR code wait for the parent phone's signed receipt and are counted in the same way. Until the receipt arrives, the child phone keeps the report's scores and sends them again in its next report.
- **Relay.** For a family paired this way the relay stores the child phone's public key (no content, no private key) and admits a child connection only with proof of that key. Other families are handled as before.
- **Camera.** Child phone only, on the scan screen only, to read the QR code. No image is stored or sent.
- **Earlier pairings.** They keep working; the parent phone shows a permanent "not verified" notice until both phones are paired again with the QR code. A child phone that is already paired needs the parent master password to pair again.
- **Not protected.** A rooted or compromised child phone can still use its key; a parent can pair a device that is not really the child's phone; a child can scan a stranger's QR code; uninstalling the app is seen only as missing heartbeats; relay metadata stays visible; the app cannot prove the key sits in tamper-resistant hardware. This is not a promise that the app is free of vulnerabilities. See [security-review.md](security-review.md).

## Scope choices and withdrawal

The teen can open **擷取範圍與停止** on the child phone and persistently stop new capture and local pending transfer, choosing to discard pending content or hold it within its original 72-hour deadline. Explicit resume discards held old pending content and restarts incoming alerts; it does not resend the old queue or restore expanded capture without renewed child acknowledgment. Saving outgoing/trusted-context choices also discards old pending content; widening a choice clears the expanded acknowledgment. Android grants are not automatically revoked by this stop control.

In the scores-only mode the teen can also pause analysis (storing and analysis stop at once, stored messages are kept, the parent is told) or withdraw (the stored chat text on the child's phone is deleted and the parent is told; alerts stay without text until the parent switches the mode off).

Both IG Guard **Notification access** and **Accessibility** can additionally be disabled in Android Settings. Turning off only one leaves the other capture path available. Permission revocation alone does not discard queues; use the in-app stop control to hold local transfer. Stopping cannot recall content already forwarded or buffered at the relay, which may still arrive, or delete delivered archives and external copies. Parents can read delivered text and choose to copy it elsewhere. Those copies are managed by whoever holds them.

## Access to parent records and keys

The parent UI requires native Android biometric/device-credential authentication and relocks when it leaves the foreground. Background delivery, decryption, archiving and signed receipts continue while that UI is locked. The app uses secure-window flags and generic private notifications, subject to Android/device behavior. Explicit app copies are marked sensitive and the app attempts to clear only its still-current clipboard item after 60 seconds; process termination, background restrictions or copying elsewhere can defeat that cleanup. Selected text copied through Android or shared outside the app is outside that timer's control. Someone who can use the phone's enrolled credentials may gain access.

Production parent-command signing uses non-exportable Android Keystore keys. Verified migration imports the existing legacy key against the child's pinned public key before removing its encoded private-key preference. If moving an existing key into the Keystore fails, the app keeps using that existing key (stored encrypted by the app, not hardware-protected), shows a warning and retries at each start; the old copy is removed only after the Keystore key is verified to match the key the child trusts. Software signing helpers used for tests are not a production storage guarantee. The parent password is still retained in encrypted preferences where existing reveal/pairing functions need it; the child stores a verifier. Hardware backing varies, and an unavailable or mismatched signing identity is not silently replaced. Routine rotation uses the existing channel and does not recover from its compromise. **家長主密碼與金鑰 → 秘密可能外洩：重新安全配對** creates fresh secrets and signing identity without sending replacement secrets over that channel. Establish that both phones are safe, stop the child's old capture locally and re-pair in person with the QR code, typing on the parent phone the 6 digits shown on the child phone. The screen can ask the relay to delete the old room first, after sending the child phone a signed release, and reports whether the relay confirmed it; if it did not, the existing pairing is kept and creating a new pairing only remains available, without relay-deletion assurance. Old local archives remain isolated; old pending content and trust settings are discarded. Copied old data cannot be recalled.

## Storage, retention and deletion

- **Parent phone.** New archive records receive a default 30-day deadline counted from when the parent's phone receives the record (the child's timestamp is display-only), with shorter retention supported. Existing archives remain without an expiry until preview and confirmed adoption; adoption can immediately expire older content. Record details offer explicit individual preservation for a one-time 90-day period from that action. Ordinary saved/bookmark or review flags do not extend retention. Cleanup occurs during app activity and archive processing; a deadline is not assurance of immediate on-disk removal while the app is stopped. Archive deletion can leave chart counts and replay-prevention identifiers. Message content and labels use device-bound Keystore AES-GCM field encryption with HMAC lookup indexes; timestamps, categories, opaque IDs and workflow flags remain visible. Preference strings, including the parent password where needed and family secret, are encrypted at rest. The app decrypts during use; Android app isolation and device security remain necessary. Losing Keystore keys can make data unreadable, without a silent reset.
- **Phone pending content.** Encrypted outboxes and incomplete assemblies receive 72-hour deadlines. Retries and routine rotation do not renew them; expiry/discard metadata prevents the same local event from being requeued. Existing pending rows get a 72-hour transition deadline on schema migration. Unaccepted or withdrawn collection holds local delivery without extending its deadline; explicit resume discards old pending content. Expired content may remain on disk until cleanup executes. Clearing app storage removes that device's queue, settings and pairing.
- **Scores-only mode on the child's phone.** The stored chat text keeps the latest 60 messages per conversation and nothing older than 14 days. It is deleted when the parent switches the mode off, the child withdraws, the model is removed, the collection method changes (when the child agrees to the new one), the child stops capture, the role or family changes, or the room is deleted. Pausing does not delete it. Score reports already queued are kept, except when the family itself ends (role change, new family, room deletion). Clearing app data or uninstalling removes the store, the agreement record and the model copy in the app's storage.
- **Scores on the parent's phone.** Score reports already received stay after any of the deletions above. They hold no message text but do hold the other person's account label, which is personal data. They expire with the archive's retention period, counted from when they were scored, and **Delete all evidence for this family** deletes them.
- **Consent receipt on each phone.** The local receipt has no automatic expiry; it persists across normal launches and updates. Clearing that phone's app data or uninstalling removes it. Archive-record deletion does not withdraw acceptance or erase the separate receipt.
- **Pairing keys.** The child phone's signing key is deleted from its Keystore when the child phone leaves the family, is reset remotely or is paired again; clearing the app's data or uninstalling also removes it. The parent phone's stored copy of the child's public key is removed when the pairing is reset or replaced, and with the family's data when the parent phone's app data is cleared. The public key stored by the relay goes with the room (see the next item). The short-lived relay room used while a QR code is shown is retired when pairing completes or the code expires.
- **Cloudflare relay.** Buffered packets expire seven days after first queuing and are removed sooner when consumed; duplicate submissions do not renew the stored deadline. Room registration metadata (parent public key, family scope, child capability hash, authorization replay state and, for a family paired by QR code, the child phone's public key) is deleted, together with any packets still waiting, when neither phone has connected for 90 consecutive days. An authenticated parent can also delete the room from the recovery screen, which reports whether the relay confirmed it. A deleted or expired room is retired for good: the relay keeps only a content-free retirement marker with no expiry, and the room, its key and the child phone's credential cannot be used again. Before deleting, the parent phone sends the child phone a signed release; a child phone that verifies it removes that room's key and unsent records, and one that is offline is cleared only when it is paired again. The parent's saved archive is kept. The relay also keeps content-free packet records (identifier hash, body hash, type and deadline) for up to 90 days after each packet's deadline. Local archive deletion does not request relay deletion, and creating a new pairing alone does not confirm it.
- **Provider and support records.** Cloudflare logging, hosting regions and backups have not been independently verified. No fixed support-correspondence retention period has been verified. Relay packet deadlines do not establish those records' lifetimes or guarantee forensic erasure.

For device controls and help with data held by the project, see the [Data Deletion Instructions](ig-guard-data-deletion.md).

## Family use and Hong Kong law

Section 52 of Hong Kong's Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance (Cap. 486) concerns personal data held by an individual and concerned only with the management of his personal, family or household affairs, or so held only for recreational purposes. It exempts qualifying data from the Data Protection Principles, Parts 4 and 5, and sections 36 and 38(b), rather than every provision of the Ordinance. Whether a particular use qualifies depends on its facts. It does not automatically exempt Safe Guard's or Cloudflare's processing. Encryption and a family-use label do not settle that assessment.

Explain actual capture and forwarding to the teen and obtain the authority and consents required for the circumstances. Other correspondents retain applicable rights and privacy interests; other laws and Instagram's contractual rules continue to matter. Neither this notice, initial acceptance, Android permission nor a scope acknowledgment is legal certification or blanket permission to process others' information. See the [Terms](ig-guard-terms-of-service.md), [storage boundaries](local-storage-security.md) and [current release notes](releases/6.0.md). Earlier data-flow reports describe their dated source versions.
