## Initial choice on each phone

Each phone displays the complete bilingual [Terms of Service](ig-guard-terms-of-service.md) offline on its initial consent page. The APK bundles the exact UTF-8 GitHub document as `ig-guard-terms-of-service.md` and displays it verbatim. The three confirmations start unticked, in this order: reading and agreeing to the full terms; conditional encrypted transfer to the dedicated verified paired parent or legal guardian with the disclosed security and retention; and authorized family-only use with the child/parent informed, without treating section 52 as automatic legal permission. Acceptance requires all three and an explicit Accept action. A separate local `ig_guard_consent` preference store holds an AES-GCM-encrypted receipt containing the acceptance date, app version, terms revision and SHA-256 of the exact terms bytes. It is not an automatic support upload or a certification of legal authority.

Collection and relay are disabled until that phone accepts. Decline or Android Back returns to Android Home without creating an acceptance receipt. Existing local queues are held pending acceptance within their deadlines, not erased by declining. Previously forwarded relay or parent copies are not deleted by declining. A parent phone and a child phone each make their own initial choice; a parent's acceptance does not provide consent on behalf of every correspondent.

Normal later launches and updates do not automatically repeat this initial page. Settings provides read-only review; materially new scope needs separate consent. Existing receipts retain their original revision and hash, not agreement to new Terms. Acceptance does not grant Android permissions, enable expanded capture or cancel a child's persistent stop choice. Pairing, Android permissions, a signed parent request and separate child-phone acknowledgment for expansion still apply. A normal update alone does not revoke an existing scope acknowledgment; stopping, resuming or widening scope requires fresh acknowledgment before expansion resumes.

## What IG Guard does

IG Guard pairs a teen’s Android phone with a dedicated parent or legal guardian’s Android phone for authorized private family safeguarding. Surveillance of strangers, partners, staff, students or clients, and commercial or institutional surveillance, is prohibited. After initial acceptance, pairing and the relevant Android permissions in Child Mode, and while collection is active, the app processes Instagram notifications and selected text exposed on the phone’s screen. It uses that information to flag selected words and messages from contacts outside the family’s approved list, forward selected records encrypted to that paired parent or legal guardian for decryption and reading, and report whether protection and delivery are working. Acceptance supplies no missing pairing or Android permission. The app uses Android services for capture. Instagram passwords and Meta API access tokens are outside this flow.

Use is limited to Instagram accounts meeting Instagram's applicable minimum age: at least 13, or higher where required. IG Guard is independent of Meta and has no Meta approval. Official Instagram API and portability integrations are not implemented.

In incoming-alert mode, the app sends qualifying incoming alerts. Expanded capture requires a signed request from the parent device and a separate acknowledgment on the teen’s device. The child can separately choose sent outgoing messages and ordinary trusted-contact context within that expanded scope. Disabling ordinary trusted context does not suppress an otherwise qualifying safety alert from a trusted contact. Composer drafts remain excluded, including comment/story-reply text and DM composer bodies; layout/hint metadata may identify a DM screen. Captures depend on the notifications and view Instagram exposes, so records may be incomplete or have uncertain author or account labels. There is no guarantee of complete capture or protection. No new model runs in the stable app; any LiquidAI/d1-omni-600M synthetic-data feasibility experiment is separate and does not establish detection efficacy.

Older builds could forward unsent comment/story-reply drafts. Those retained records remain labelled historical, with sending unconfirmed. The update does not automatically delete them; normal archive deletion controls still apply.

## Scope choices and withdrawal

The teen can open **擷取範圍與停止** on the child phone and persistently stop new capture and local pending transfer, choosing to discard pending content or hold it within its original 72-hour deadline. Explicit resume discards held old pending content and restarts incoming alerts; it does not resend the old queue or restore expanded capture without renewed child acknowledgment. Saving outgoing/trusted-context choices also discards old pending content; widening a choice clears the expanded acknowledgment. Android grants are not automatically revoked by this stop control.

Both IG Guard **Notification access** and **Accessibility** can additionally be disabled in Android Settings. Turning off only one leaves the other capture path available. Permission revocation alone does not discard queues; use the in-app stop control to hold local transfer. Stopping cannot recall content already forwarded or buffered at the relay, which may still arrive, or delete delivered archives and external copies. Parents can read delivered text and choose to copy it elsewhere. Those copies are managed by whoever holds them.

## Access to parent records and keys

The parent UI requires native Android biometric/device-credential authentication and relocks when it leaves the foreground. Background delivery, decryption, archiving and signed receipts continue while that UI is locked. The app uses secure-window flags and generic private notifications, subject to Android/device behavior. Explicit app copies are marked sensitive and the app attempts to clear only its still-current clipboard item after 60 seconds; process termination, background restrictions or copying elsewhere can defeat that cleanup. Selected text copied through Android or shared outside the app is outside that timer's control. Someone who can use the phone's enrolled credentials may gain access.

Production parent-command signing uses non-exportable Android Keystore keys. Verified migration imports the existing legacy key against the child's pinned public key before removing its encoded private-key preference. If moving an existing key into the Keystore fails, the app keeps using that existing key (stored encrypted by the app, not hardware-protected), shows a warning and retries at each start; the old copy is removed only after the Keystore key is verified to match the key the child trusts. Software signing helpers used for tests are not a production storage guarantee. The parent password is still retained in encrypted preferences where existing reveal/pairing functions need it; the child stores a verifier. Hardware backing varies, and an unavailable or mismatched signing identity is not silently replaced. Routine rotation uses the existing channel and does not recover from its compromise. **家長主密碼與金鑰 → 秘密可能外洩：重新安全配對** creates fresh secrets and signing identity without sending replacement secrets over that channel. Establish that both phones are safe, stop the child's old capture locally and re-pair in person with full security-code verification. The screen can ask the relay to delete the old room first, after sending the child phone a signed release, and reports whether the relay confirmed it; if it did not, the existing pairing is kept and creating a new pairing only remains available, without relay-deletion assurance. Old local archives remain isolated; old pending content and trust settings are discarded. Copied old data cannot be recalled.

## Storage, retention and deletion

- **Parent phone.** New archive records receive a default 30-day deadline counted from when the parent's phone receives the record (the child's timestamp is display-only), with shorter retention supported. Existing archives remain without an expiry until preview and confirmed adoption; adoption can immediately expire older content. Record details offer explicit individual preservation for a one-time 90-day period from that action. Ordinary saved/bookmark or review flags do not extend retention. Cleanup occurs during app activity and archive processing; a deadline is not assurance of immediate on-disk removal while the app is stopped. Archive deletion can leave chart counts and replay-prevention identifiers. Message content and labels use device-bound Keystore AES-GCM field encryption with HMAC lookup indexes; timestamps, categories, opaque IDs and workflow flags remain visible. Preference strings, including the parent password where needed and family secret, are encrypted at rest. The app decrypts during use; Android app isolation and device security remain necessary. Losing Keystore keys can make data unreadable, without a silent reset.
- **Phone pending content.** Encrypted outboxes and incomplete assemblies receive 72-hour deadlines. Retries and routine rotation do not renew them; expiry/discard metadata prevents the same local event from being requeued. Existing pending rows get a 72-hour transition deadline on schema migration. Unaccepted or withdrawn collection holds local delivery without extending its deadline; explicit resume discards old pending content. Expired content may remain on disk until cleanup executes. Clearing app storage removes that device's queue, settings and pairing.
- **Consent receipt on each phone.** The local receipt has no automatic expiry; it persists across normal launches and updates. Clearing that phone's app data or uninstalling removes it. Archive-record deletion does not withdraw acceptance or erase the separate receipt.
- **Cloudflare relay.** Buffered packets expire seven days after first queuing and are removed sooner when consumed; duplicate submissions do not renew the stored deadline. Room registration metadata (parent public key, family scope, child capability hash and authorization replay state) is deleted, together with any packets still waiting, when neither phone has connected for 90 consecutive days. An authenticated parent can also delete the room from the recovery screen, which reports whether the relay confirmed it. A deleted or expired room is retired for good: the relay keeps only a content-free retirement marker with no expiry, and the room, its key and the child phone's credential cannot be used again. Before deleting, the parent phone sends the child phone a signed release; a child phone that verifies it removes that room's key and unsent records, and one that is offline is cleared only when it is paired again. The parent's saved archive is kept. The relay also keeps content-free packet records (identifier hash, body hash, type and deadline) for up to 90 days after each packet's deadline. Local archive deletion does not request relay deletion, and creating a new pairing alone does not confirm it.
- **Provider and support records.** Cloudflare logging, hosting regions and backups have not been independently verified. No fixed support-correspondence retention period has been verified. Relay packet deadlines do not establish those records' lifetimes or guarantee forensic erasure.

For device controls and help with data held by the project, see the [Data Deletion Instructions](ig-guard-data-deletion.md).

## Family use and Hong Kong law

Section 52 of Hong Kong's Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance (Cap. 486) concerns personal data held by an individual and concerned only with the management of his personal, family or household affairs, or so held only for recreational purposes. It exempts qualifying data from the Data Protection Principles, Parts 4 and 5, and sections 36 and 38(b), rather than every provision of the Ordinance. Whether a particular use qualifies depends on its facts. It does not automatically exempt Safe Guard's or Cloudflare's processing. Encryption and a family-use label do not settle that assessment.

Explain actual capture and forwarding to the teen and obtain the authority and consents required for the circumstances. Other correspondents retain applicable rights and privacy interests; other laws and Instagram's contractual rules continue to matter. Neither this notice, initial acceptance, Android permission nor a scope acknowledgment is legal certification or blanket permission to process others' information. See the [Terms](ig-guard-terms-of-service.md), [storage boundaries](local-storage-security.md) and [current draft notes](releases/4.9-beta.4.md). Earlier data-flow reports describe their dated source versions.
